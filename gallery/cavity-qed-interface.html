Quantum interface

Cavity QED Interface

From gold-coated fiber mirrors to needle-trap integration: building high-bandwidth, high-efficiency ion–photon interfaces.

We integrate fiber microcavities with needle ion traps to develop ion–photon interfaces for quantum networks. The small cavity mode volume enhances ion–cavity coupling, while the fibers provide channels for collecting and transmitting photons. By designing the mirrors, trapping geometry and optical coupling together, we aim to build network nodes that combine high bandwidth with high efficiency.

The photographs show the hardware behind this interface. Gold coating on the fiber sides and the outer regions of the end faces provides electrical shielding while leaving the central optical apertures open. Two opposing fiber mirrors form a microcavity, which is integrated with the needle trap inside the vacuum chamber. The fluorescence image of a single Ba⁺ ion between the mirrors shows the ion confined within this cavity geometry.
