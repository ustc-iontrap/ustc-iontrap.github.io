"An Ultraviolet Fiber Fabry–Pérot Cavity for Florescence Collection of Trapped Ions" is published

News · 2017-01-22 · qng

Our work “An Ultraviolet Fiber Fabry–Pérot Cavity for Florescence Collection of Trapped Ions” has been published in Chinese Physics Letters. We demonstrate a fiber Fabry–Pérot cavity (FFPC) operating in the ultraviolet range at 369 nm, specifically designed for enhancing fluorescence collection from trapped 171Yb+ ions — a key technology for building scalable quantum networks.

Why Fiber Cavities?

Trapped ions are an ideal platform for quantum information processing, but building a large-scale quantum computer and long-distance quantum communication requires the ability to remotely distribute entanglement between ions. The key ingredient is efficient photon collection. A fiber Fabry–Pérot cavity can provide a large Purcell factor to enhance the photon emission rate while maintaining high coupling efficiency between the single fiber mode and the cavity mode — solving the long-standing problem of low photon collection efficiency.

Technical Achievements

We designed the FFPC at the bad cavity limit for fluorescence enhancement at the 2P1/2 to 2S1/2 transition wavelength of Yb+. The concave mirror profiles on fiber-end faces were fabricated using CO2 laser machining with spatial filtering, achieving an averaged eccentricity of only 0.22 ± 0.04. The mirrors were coated for high reflection in the ultraviolet range by ion-beam sputtering, achieving a coating reflectivity of 99.76% and a finesse of 1300 ± 100.

Key Results

Based on measured experimental parameters and numerical simulation, we estimate:

  • Purcell factor > 2.5
  • Single-mode fiber collection efficiency > 10%

These results demonstrate the FFPC’s potential to entangle remote ions, which is the basis for constructing quantum networks and developing distributed quantum computation.
